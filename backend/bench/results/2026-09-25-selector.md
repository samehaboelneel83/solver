# The learned solver selector in shadow mode — 2026-09-25

`python -m bench.selector --time-limit 10 --write` (queue R11). Every bench
family, sizes M and L, instances 0-2; every backend the rules admit for each
model, solved in a sandboxed child; the fastest to prove the optimum is the
example's answer (`app/solve/selector_data.json`, 66 models, 50 with a
proof). Scored on families it has not seen: each family predicted from the
others' examples only (leave one family out), five nearest neighbours.

**Verdict: shadow mode only -- it records, it never acts.**

- The rules' pick was the fastest on 16 of 50 (32%); the selector's on 33
  (66%). Shifted geometric mean time: rules 1.05 s, selector 0.94 s, the
  best possible 0.69 s.
- But its *confident* picks (vote >= 80%) were right on only 16 of 25: on
  feed_blend it votes SCIP at 100% where GLOP is far faster -- no family it
  has seen is a plain linear program, so its neighbours mislead it. A pick
  that confident and that wrong is exactly why it does not act.
- Many of these times are under a second, where starting a sandboxed child
  weighs as much as the solve: the rules' choice is not slow, only often not
  the fastest.

Every run now records the selector's pick beside the solver that ran
(`params.selector`: pick, vote share, the families it resembles, agree or
not). The case for letting it act is `run_fact` holding enough real runs to
train and check on this platform's own models -- not the bench's.
