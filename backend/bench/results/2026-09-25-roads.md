# Road distances from the tile server's roads -- 2026-09-25 (queue R16b)

No OpenStreetMap extract is on this machine and the solver's Postgres has
no PostGIS or pgRouting; the tile server's `egypt_osm` vector tiles
(OpenMapTiles, zoom 12, `transportation`: motorway to minor) were chosen
over a database image swap. `app.spatial.mvt` decodes them (checked against
the reference `mapbox-vector-tile` library on the densest central Cairo
tile: 2,142 of 2,142 features identical; that library itself was not used,
as it pins a protobuf OR-Tools cannot run with).

Measured on 60 random places in a 12 km x 11 km box of central Cairo (nine
tiles), 20 x 40 pairs, road distance against the straight line:

| graph | pieces | largest piece | road / straight: median | p90 | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| vertices joined only where the tiles share them | 56,086 | 12% | 2.23 | -- | 52.1 |
| + dead ends joined within 20 m | 17,267 | 64.5% | 1.60 | 2.32 | 11.9 |
| + every at-grade crossing noded (bridges and tunnels not), dead ends joined to the nearest road | 55 | 99.8% | **1.30** | **1.75** | 6.3 |

**Verdict: the noded graph ships.** Zoom-12 simplification drops the
vertex two crossing streets share, so the tiles alone are a pile of
disconnected lines; noding them (shapely) and joining dead ends makes a
network whose detours look like a city's (a median 1.30 is the usual urban
circuity). Its limits are recorded in every value's `source`: the zoom-12
roads (small streets simplified or missing), every road both ways (the
tiles' `oneway` marks 2,105 of 2,142 lines, so it is not read), free-flow
speeds by class (median trip 48.7 km/h), no traffic. A place over 2 km from
the main network, or on another piece of it, has no road distance: its
pairs are left to the parameter's recorded far default and counted
(`no_road`), never estimated.

Building the graph for nine tiles takes about 20 s (the noding); the
shortest paths from 20 origins, 0.1 s. A request reads at most 36 tiles
(about 60 km square).
