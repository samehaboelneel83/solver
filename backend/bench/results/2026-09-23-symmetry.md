for each instance, the classes of interchangeable entities (`app.solve.symmetry.classes`) and, on HiGHS and
the MILP wrapper (the backends the rows are for), the solve with the ordering rows off and on.

(3 classes of 10 at M, of 26-27 at L); `rota` draws each person's cap and cost, so classes are pairs and
triples; `facility` has none -- the control.

## Decision: `solve.symmetry` stays off by default

- **Never wrong.** Every one of the 36 pairs proved the same optimum (and facility L 2 on the MILP wrapper
  reached the same answer unproven both ways); `tests/test_symmetry.py` checks 30 random rosters against
  CP-SAT with no rows.
- **Never faster where it matters.** On `rota_teams`, where it has the most to cut, the rows make the MILP
  wrapper 2.6x slower by SGM (0.170 s to 0.439 s, slower on all six) and HiGHS 8% slower. These solvers
  already handle this symmetry themselves -- HiGHS and SCIP (the MILP wrapper's engine) detect orbits in
  presolve -- and extra rows only weaken their own reasoning and add work. On `facility`, with no classes,
  it is a no-op, as it should be.
- **So it stays a technique behind a setting.** A problem whose solver does not see its symmetry (CBC, or a
  model too large for presolve's orbit detection) can turn it on at its own level and measure; nothing in
  the families here is one.
